<div>

<h2>What is this project?</h2>

<p>A simple economic explanation of the industrial waste problem.</p>

</div>

</div>

<div class="grid-2">

<div class="card">

<h3>🏭 The basic problem</h3>

<p>

Imagine an industrial factory producing goods. During

production, the factory also produces waste.

</p>

<br>

<p>

The factory has to decide what to do with that waste:

it can recycle or treat it, which may cost money, or

dispose of it.

</p>

</div>

<div class="card">

<h3>📈 Where economics comes in</h3>

<p>

The factory looks at its own costs when making this decision.

But waste and pollution can also create costs for people,

communities and the environment.

</p>

<br>

<p>

Economics helps us understand these costs and how government

policies can change the incentives facing the factory.

</p>

</div>

</div>

</section>

<!-- =========================================================

GOVERNMENT POLICIES

========================================================= -->

<section>

<div class="section-title">

<span>🏛️</span>

<div>

<h2>How can the Government influence this?</h2>

<p>

Government can change the economic incentives faced by producers.

</p>

</div>

</div>

<div class="policy-grid">

<div class="policy-card">

<div class="policy-icon">🧾</div>

<h3>Pollution Tax</h3>

<p>

The government can impose a tax on pollution or waste disposal.

This increases the cost of polluting and can encourage factories

to reduce waste or choose cleaner alternatives.

</p>

<div class="example">

<strong>Example:</strong>

A factory pays ₹200 per tonne of taxable waste disposal.

</div>

</div>

<div class="policy-card">

<div class="policy-icon">♻️</div>

<h3>Recycling Subsidy</h3>

<p>

The government can provide financial support for recycling

or cleaner production. This lowers the effective cost of

environmentally friendly activities.

</p>

<div class="example">

<strong>Example:</strong>

A factory receives ₹100 per tonne of qualifying recycled material.

</div>

</div>

</div>

</section>

<!-- =========================================================

SOCIAL COST EXPLANATION

========================================================= -->

<section>

<div class="section-title">

<span>👥</span>

<div>

<h2>Why does Social Cost matter to the public?</h2>

<p>

Pollution can create costs for people who are not involved

in the factory's decision.

</p>

</div>

</div>

<div class="public-cost-box">

<div class="public-intro">

<strong>

🏭 Factory decision → 🌫️ Pollution → 👥 Public impact

</strong>

<p>

The factory may only pay the direct cost of disposing of its

waste. However, pollution can create additional costs for

society. These are called <strong>external costs</strong>.

</p>

</div>

<div class="impact-grid">

<div class="impact-card">

<span>🌫️</span>

<h3>Health</h3>

<p>

Pollution can increase health-related costs for people

living near affected areas.

</p>

</div>

<div class="impact-card">

<span>💧</span>

<h3>Water & Soil</h3>

<p>

Waste can damage water sources, soil quality and other

resources used by communities.

</p>

</div>

<div class="impact-card">

<span>🧹</span>

<h3>Public Cleanup</h3>

<p>

Governments may have to spend public money managing

pollution and cleaning affected areas.

</p>

</div>

<div class="impact-card">

<span>🌱</span>

<h3>Environment</h3>

<p>

Pollution can cause environmental damage that does not

appear on the factory's own balance sheet.

</p>

</div>

</div>

<div class="social-cost-explanation">

<h3>💡 Why is this useful?</h3>

<p>

Social cost helps governments understand the

<strong>broader economic impact</strong> of pollution.

If a factory considers only its private cost, it may have

less incentive to reduce waste.

</p>

<br>

<p>

By considering the costs imposed on the public, policymakers

can design measures such as pollution taxes, recycling

subsidies and environmental regulations.

</p>

<div class="formula-large">

<span>Social Cost</span>

<b>=</b>

<span>Private Cost</span>

<b>+</b>

<span>External Cost on Society</span>

</div>

<div class="example-highlight">

<strong>Our example:</strong>

The factory's disposal cost is

<strong>₹100/tonne</strong>.

The estimated external cost to society is

<strong>₹250/tonne</strong>.

<br><br>

Therefore:

<br>

<strong>₹100 + ₹250 = ₹350 social cost per tonne</strong>

</div>

<p class="small-note">

The ₹250 is not a direct bill paid by each citizen.

It is an illustrative economic estimate of the harm and

costs that pollution can impose on society.

</p>

</div>

</div>

</section>

<h2>♻️ From Waste to Wealth</h2>

<p>

Waste is not only an environmental issue — it is also an

economic issue. Factories respond to costs and incentives.

Governments can influence those incentives through policies

such as pollution taxes and recycling subsidies.

</p>

<br>

<p>

When the economic costs imposed on society are considered,

businesses have a stronger reason to reduce waste, recycle

materials and adopt cleaner production methods.

</p>

<br>

<p>

The ultimate idea is simple:

<strong>

instead of seeing waste only as something to throw away,

we can look for ways to turn it into an economic resource.

</strong>

const taxSlider = document.getElementById("tax");

const subsidySlider = document.getElementById("subsidy");

const taxValue = document.getElementById("taxValue");

const subsidyValue = document.getElementById("subsidyValue");

const recyclingRateElement =

document.getElementById("recyclingRate");

const disposedElement =

document.getElementById("disposed");

const recycledBar =

document.getElementById("recycledBar");

const disposedBar =

document.getElementById("disposedBar");

const recycledLegend =

document.getElementById("recycledLegend");

const disposedLegend =

document.getElementById("disposedLegend");

const socialCostElement =

document.getElementById("socialCost");

const taxExplanation =

document.getElementById("taxExplanation");

const insightText =

document.getElementById("insightText");

/*

UPDATE SIMULATOR

*/

function updateSimulator() {

const tax = Number(taxSlider.value);

const subsidy = Number(subsidySlider.value);

/*

Simplified behavioural relationship:

Every ₹100 of combined policy incentive

increases the recycling rate by approximately

4 percentage points.

The rate is restricted between 10% and 90%.

This is only an educational assumption.

*/

let recyclingRate =

baseRecyclingRate +

((tax + subsidy) / 100) * 4;

recyclingRate =

Math.max(

10,

Math.min(90, recyclingRate)

);

recyclingRate =

Math.round(recyclingRate);

const recycled =

Math.round(

totalWaste * recyclingRate / 100

);

const disposed =

totalWaste - recycled;

/*

SOCIAL COST

Social cost here is:

Private disposal cost

+

External environmental cost

The pollution tax is shown separately because

a tax is a policy transfer/incentive rather than

automatically being an additional real resource

cost to society.

*/

const socialCost =

privateDisposalCost +

externalCost;

/*

UPDATE TEXT

*/

taxValue.textContent = tax;

subsidyValue.textContent = subsidy;

recyclingRateElement.textContent =

recyclingRate + "%";

disposedElement.textContent =

disposed;

/*

UPDATE BAR GRAPH

*/

recycledBar.style.width =

recyclingRate + "%";

disposedBar.style.width =

(100 - recyclingRate) + "%";

/*

UPDATE LEGENDS

*/

recycledLegend.textContent =

recycled + " t";

disposedLegend.textContent =

disposed + " t";

/*

UPDATE SOCIAL COST

*/

socialCostElement.textContent =

socialCost;

/*

TAX EXPLANATION

*/

if (tax === 0) {

taxExplanation.textContent =

"The tax is currently ₹0, so the firm's disposal decision is not affected by a pollution tax.";

} else {

taxExplanation.textContent =

"A ₹" + tax +

"/tonne pollution tax increases the firm's effective cost of disposal and creates an incentive to reduce waste.";

}

/*

ECONOMIC INSIGHT

*/

if (tax === 0 && subsidy === 0) {

insightText.textContent =

"With no pollution tax and no recycling subsidy, the model starts with a 40% recycling rate. The remaining 60% is disposed of.";

}

else if (tax > 0 && subsidy === 0) {

insightText.textContent =

"The pollution tax makes disposal more expensive for the factory. In this simplified model, that stronger incentive increases the recycling rate.";

}

else if (tax === 0 && subsidy > 0) {

insightText.textContent =

"The recycling subsidy lowers the effective cost of recycling. In this simplified model, the factory therefore has a stronger incentive to recycle.";

}

else {

insightText.textContent =

"Both policies are being used. The pollution tax raises the cost of disposal while the recycling subsidy makes recycling more attractive. Together, they create a stronger incentive to reduce waste.";

}

}

/*

LISTEN FOR SLIDER CHANGES

*/

taxSlider.addEventListener(

"input",

updateSimulator

);

subsidySlider.addEventListener(

"input",

updateSimulator

);

/*

INITIAL DISPLAY

*/

updateSimulator();

</script>
